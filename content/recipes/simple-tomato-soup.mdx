## Why this recipe

Most cooking teaches you to taste at the end, when the only remaining decision
is how much salt to add. This soup is built so that tasting is the *technique*,
not the last step.

The three marked tastings are not decoration. The soup genuinely tastes like
three different things at those points, and the gap between the second and third
is the clearest demonstration available of what simmering actually does.

## What is happening between tastings

**After the onions.** You have built a sweet, savory base. Onion cooked slowly
without browning goes from harsh and sulfurous to genuinely sweet — those
compounds broke down and sugars became perceptible. Nothing else in the soup will
add this, so if it is bland here, the finished soup will be bland.

**Just after the tomatoes go in.** Sharp and separate. Canned tomatoes are
noticeably acidic, and nothing has integrated. Cooks who season heavily at this
point end up with oversalted soup, because there is still a lot of water to
evaporate.

**After the simmer.** Two things happened. Water left, so everything
concentrated. And the harsh volatile compounds in raw tomato cooked off while
sugars developed. The soup tastes rounder and deeper, and it tastes *less* acidic
than it did — not because acid left, but because sweetness and body came up to
meet it.

## The diagnosis at the end

Run the table from the tasting lesson:

| If it tastes | Add |
| --- | --- |
| Flat, like nothing | Salt, in small increments |
| Sharp, aggressive, sour | A little sugar, or butter or cream |
| Thin and hollow | A parmesan rind during the simmer, or a splash of fish sauce |
| Heavy, dull, tiring | A small splash of vinegar — yes, even here |
| Fine but boring | Something on top: olive oil, croutons, basil, chile |

That last row is the one people skip. A correctly seasoned soup with nothing on
top is monotonous by the fourth spoonful. Texture contrast is a real fix.

## Variations

- **Tomato and bread**: stir in torn stale bread and let it collapse. This is
  approximately pappa al pomodoro and it is better than this recipe.
- **Roasted**: roast the canned tomatoes on a sheet pan at 425F for 25 minutes
  first. Browning adds a dimension simmering cannot.
- **Coconut and chile**: swap cream for coconut milk, add chile and ginger with
  the garlic.
- **With a parmesan rind**: drop one in during the simmer and fish it out.
  Umami, free, from something you were going to throw away.

## What goes wrong

- **Browned onions.** Not wrong, but a different soup — sweeter and deeper. Do it
  on purpose or not at all.
- **Burnt garlic.** One minute, medium heat. It goes bitter fast and the
  bitterness never leaves.
- **Seasoned early and now oversalted.** Reduction concentrates salt. Season in
  small amounts across the cook.
- **Blender eruption.** Hot liquid expands violently. Vent the lid, cover with a
  towel, fill the jar half full.
- **Tastes like canned tomatoes.** It did not simmer long enough. That is the
  whole point of the 30 minutes.
